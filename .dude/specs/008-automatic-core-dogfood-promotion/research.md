## Why Replacement Or Rebaseline Is Rejected

A replacement baseline is rejected for four independent reasons:

1. **Temporal invalidity:** a baseline captured after source mutation cannot prove a clean pre-source boundary.
2. **Parity failure:** the known attempted replacement lacked its mandatory parity precondition and is explicitly void.
3. **Changed-set erasure:** moving the base after accepted source changes can turn the changed set empty and bypass declaration and materialization gates.
4. **Authority expansion:** allowing replacement would generalize an exception to later features and permit retroactive interval creation.

The bridge therefore never appends, repairs, replaces, transplants, or selects a later baseline. A stale or void line remains audit history only.

## Why Descendant Plus Explicit Authorization Is Required

Descendant proof establishes that current history continues from the original baseline rather than switching to an unrelated line. It does not establish that intervening revision movement was permitted.

Explicit authorization evidence establishes the human-approved commit or merge continuity. It does not establish ancestry or preserve source and generated identities.

Both are required. Requiring only ancestry would admit unauthorized commits; requiring only authorization would admit an unrelated revision. The remaining source, declaration, generated-prestate, protected-boundary, and authority gates prove that the permitted history movement did not change the accepted contents or interval meaning.
